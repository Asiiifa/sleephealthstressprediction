SLEEP HEALTH AND STRESS - KNOWLEDGE BASE
==========================================

TOPIC: Sleep Duration and Health
Sleep duration directly affects physical and mental health. Adults need 7-9 hours of sleep per night. Sleeping less than 6 hours increases risk of heart disease, diabetes, and obesity. Chronic sleep deprivation weakens the immune system and impairs cognitive function. Consistent sleep schedule — sleeping and waking at the same time daily — improves sleep quality significantly.

TOPIC: Sleep Quality vs Sleep Duration
Sleep quality matters as much as duration. Poor quality sleep — frequent waking, light sleep — leaves a person feeling tired even after 8 hours. Deep sleep (slow-wave sleep) is essential for physical recovery. REM sleep is essential for memory consolidation and emotional regulation. Factors affecting sleep quality include stress, screen time, caffeine, and room temperature.

TOPIC: Stress and Sleep Relationship
Stress and sleep have a bidirectional relationship. High stress causes poor sleep, and poor sleep increases stress. Cortisol — the stress hormone — is naturally low at night to allow sleep. Chronic stress keeps cortisol elevated, making it difficult to fall asleep. Managing stress through relaxation techniques improves sleep quality significantly.

TOPIC: Stress Levels and Physical Health
Chronic high stress leads to hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and weakened immunity. Stress triggers the fight-or-flight response, increasing heart rate and blood pressure. Long-term activation of stress response damages the cardiovascular system. High stress is linked to digestive problems, headaches, and muscle tension. Early stress management prevents long-term health complications.

TOPIC: Physical Activity and Sleep
Regular physical activity improves sleep quality and reduces stress. Exercise increases deep sleep duration. Morning or afternoon exercise is most beneficial for sleep. Exercising too close to bedtime (within 2 hours) can disrupt sleep by raising body temperature and adrenaline. Even 30 minutes of moderate activity like walking improves sleep and reduces stress hormones.

TOPIC: Daily Steps and Health
Walking 7,000-10,000 steps daily is associated with better sleep and lower stress. Physical inactivity increases cortisol levels and worsens sleep quality. Regular walking reduces anxiety and depression symptoms. Even 20-minute daily walks significantly improve mood and energy levels. Low daily steps combined with sedentary lifestyle increases risk of sleep disorders.

TOPIC: Heart Rate and Stress
Resting heart rate is a key indicator of stress and cardiovascular health. Normal resting heart rate is 60-100 bpm. A resting heart rate above 80 bpm may indicate elevated stress or poor cardiovascular fitness. High stress increases resting heart rate. Regular exercise lowers resting heart rate over time. Monitoring resting heart rate helps track stress levels and recovery.

TOPIC: Caffeine and Sleep
Caffeine is a stimulant that blocks adenosine — the sleep-promoting chemical in the brain. Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours — half remains in the body after that time. Consuming caffeine after 2 PM significantly disrupts nighttime sleep quality. More than 4 cups of coffee per day is linked to increased anxiety and poor sleep. Reducing caffeine intake, especially in the afternoon, improves sleep onset and quality.

TOPIC: Screen Time and Sleep
Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin production — the hormone that signals sleep. Using phones, laptops, or tablets within 1 hour of bedtime delays sleep onset by 1-2 hours. Screen time before bed increases brain alertness, making it harder to fall asleep. Reducing screen time at night significantly improves sleep quality and duration. Blue light filtering glasses or night mode settings reduce but do not eliminate this effect.

TOPIC: Sleep Deprivation Warning Signs
Warning signs of dangerous sleep deprivation include difficulty concentrating, memory problems, and mood swings. Severe sleep deprivation causes hallucinations, impaired judgment, and increased accident risk. Regularly sleeping less than 6 hours increases risk of depression by 5 times. Sleep deprivation affects metabolism, increasing hunger hormones and risk of weight gain. Persistent sleep problems require medical attention — they may indicate sleep apnea or insomnia.

TOPIC: Relaxation Techniques for Better Sleep
Deep breathing exercises activate the parasympathetic nervous system, reducing stress and promoting sleep. Progressive muscle relaxation — tensing and releasing muscle groups — reduces physical tension before sleep. Meditation and mindfulness reduce cortisol levels and improve sleep quality. The 4-7-8 breathing technique — inhale 4 seconds, hold 7, exhale 8 — promotes rapid relaxation. Body scan meditation helps release tension accumulated during the day.

TOPIC: Sleep Environment
Ideal sleep temperature is 18-20 degrees Celsius. Dark rooms increase melatonin production. Noise disrupts sleep cycles — even if it does not fully wake the person. Comfortable mattress and pillow are essential for physical relaxation. Keeping bedroom exclusively for sleep — not work or screens — trains the brain to associate bed with sleep.

TOPIC: Stress Management Techniques
Regular exercise is one of the most effective stress reducers — it releases endorphins. Time management and breaking tasks into smaller steps reduces overwhelm and anxiety. Social support — talking to friends and family — significantly reduces stress levels. Journaling — writing down worries — externalizes stress and reduces mental load. Professional help through therapy or counseling is recommended for chronic high stress.

TOPIC: Sleep Score Interpretation
A sleep score of 80-100 indicates excellent sleep health — deep, restorative sleep. A score of 60-79 indicates moderate sleep health — some improvements needed. A score of 40-59 indicates poor sleep — immediate lifestyle changes required. A score below 40 indicates critical sleep deprivation — serious health risk. Improving sleep score requires consistent sleep schedule, reduced screen time, and stress management.

TOPIC: Stress Score Interpretation
A stress score of 0-30 indicates low stress — healthy and manageable. A score of 31-60 indicates moderate stress — requires attention and management strategies. A score of 61-80 indicates high stress — significant lifestyle changes needed. A score above 80 indicates critical stress levels — professional help recommended. Chronic high stress without intervention leads to burnout, anxiety disorders, and physical illness.

TOPIC: Sleep and Mental Health
Poor sleep significantly worsens anxiety and depression symptoms. Sleep deprivation reduces emotional regulation, making stress feel more overwhelming. REM sleep processes emotional memories, helping the brain recover from stressful events. Improving sleep quality is one of the first recommendations for managing anxiety and depression. Sleep and mental health form a cycle — addressing one improves the other.

TOPIC: Naps and Sleep Health
Short naps of 10-20 minutes improve alertness and reduce stress without disrupting nighttime sleep. Naps longer than 30 minutes can cause sleep inertia — grogginess upon waking. Napping after 3 PM disrupts nighttime sleep onset. Power naps are beneficial for people with sleep debt. However, relying on naps to compensate for chronic sleep deprivation is not a sustainable solution.

TOPIC: Age and Sleep Needs
Adults aged 18-64 need 7-9 hours of sleep. Older adults aged 65+ need 7-8 hours. Sleep architecture changes with age — less deep sleep, more frequent waking. Young adults often accumulate sleep debt due to irregular schedules and late nights. Prioritizing sleep becomes more important with age as recovery takes longer.

TOPIC: Nutrition and Sleep
Heavy meals within 2-3 hours of bedtime disrupt sleep quality. Foods rich in tryptophan — turkey, milk, bananas — promote serotonin and melatonin production. Alcohol may help with initial sleep onset but disrupts sleep cycles and reduces sleep quality. Staying hydrated improves sleep quality — but avoid excessive fluids before bed to prevent waking. Magnesium-rich foods — nuts, seeds, leafy greens — support muscle relaxation and better sleep.

TOPIC: Work-Life Balance and Stress
Poor work-life balance is a leading cause of chronic stress. Setting clear boundaries between work and personal time reduces cortisol levels. Taking regular breaks during work — every 90 minutes — improves focus and reduces stress accumulation. Overworking without rest leads to burnout — a state of complete mental and physical exhaustion. Scheduling leisure activities and hobbies is as important as scheduling work tasks.
